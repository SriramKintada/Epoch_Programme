The Problem

Most programs end with a presentation.
We end with revenue.

India does not have a talent problem. It does not have an ideas problem. What it has is an execution problem — too many brilliant engineers and researchers stuck in planning mode, waiting for the perfect conditions that never arrive.

Conventional accelerators offer months of workshops, culminating in a demo day where founders pitch to mismatched investors. Most leave with nothing but a polished slide deck.

FATHOM is built on a different principle: intense, focused execution over four weekends, with every milestone tied directly to market validation and revenue. We back builders across AI and deeptech — from applied ML and automation to frontier hardware and beyond.

How It Works

Not a demo day.
A market event.

Most accelerators end with founders pitching to a room of investors who are not matched to their domain. The result is misaligned conversations and, for most founders, no meaningful outcome. A single exit path: investment, or nothing.

FATHOM's final event brings together two groups:

Investors

Pre-selected to match the specific domains of the startups in the cohort. No misaligned conversations.

Acquirers

Companies and domain players who may want to hire, acquire, or partner with the teams being built.

Four outcome pathways. Not one.

The Competitive Dynamic

When investors and acquirers are both present, something significant happens. If an acquirer signals interest in purchasing a startup, investors recognise the underlying value — and may choose to compete by offering capital instead, encouraging the founders to scale before any acquisition. This creates a market-driven environment where founders hold leverage, and the likelihood of better terms — through either investment or acquisition — increases substantially.

Interview Details

Cohort conversations.

This is not a formal HR interview. We are trying to understand default agency, speed, honesty, and whether you will push hard with or without external structure.

Best practices adapted from YC style founder and startup interviews: keep it direct, specific, evidence-based, and grounded in real work. We care less about polished answers and more about proof of initiative, clarity of thought, and whether you naturally move toward users and revenue.

1. What we are evaluating +
Proactiveness Promise of proactiveness Default energy GTM orientation Community leverage Product taste

We are looking for people who do not wait to be managed. If you join EPOCH, will you move by default? Will you talk to users, ship uncomfortable things, ask for help fast, and use the community as a force multiplier instead of as a social club?

2. Core interview questions +
  • What are you building or what problem are you obsessed with right now?
  • Why this problem, and why now?
  • What have you already done without being asked?
  • Tell us about the strongest proof that you can execute fast.
  • If we gave you the next 4 weekends, what exactly would you try to get done?
  • Who is the user, and what pain are they already feeling today?
  • How would you get your first 10 users without paid ads?
  • What part of GTM are you willing to do yourself?
  • How would you use the community here instead of working alone?
  • Why do you think EPOCH is the right place for you specifically?
3. Default energy and proactiveness checks +
  • Tell us about a time you created momentum from zero.
  • What have you pursued recently that nobody externally forced you to pursue?
  • When you get blocked, what do you do in the next 30 minutes?
  • What is one uncomfortable but necessary thing you would do for growth?
  • If nobody followed up with you after today, what would you do next?
4. GTM and market pull questions +
  • How would you get distribution in week 1, not month 6?
  • What channel feels most unfairly accessible to you right now?
  • Who can you message today to create demand?
  • What would you sell before the product is fully ready?
  • What kind of help from the cohort would actually speed you up?
  • What would be your first revenue experiment?
5. Product and past work questions +
  • Show us something you built, shipped, sold, or ran.
  • What was hard about it, and what part did you personally own?
  • What did you learn from a project that did not work?
  • What is your edge: speed, insight, distribution, technical depth, taste, or something else?
  • What are you unusually good at relative to people around you?
6. Interviewer guide +
  • Push for specifics. Ask for names, dates, users, numbers, and examples.
  • Reward clarity and honesty over polished storytelling.
  • Prefer evidence of action over ambition alone.
  • Look for candidates who already have momentum, not just ideas.
  • End with: what will you do in the next 7 days if selected?
